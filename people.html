People

The founders and partners behind Y Combinator

PG

Paul Graham

Co-founder and public voice (2005–2014)
2005–2014

Paul Graham is a programmer, essayist, and Lisp expert who co-founded Y Combinator in 2005 after selling his startup Viaweb to Yahoo. He designed the batch-funding model and, through his widely read essays, supplied YC's intellectual framework and public credibility for its first decade.

His essays — and the mantra 'Make something people want' — became required reading across the startup world.
JL

Jessica Livingston

Co-founder; head of culture and operations (2005–2015)
2005–2015

Jessica Livingston is a writer and startup community organizer who co-founded Y Combinator in 2005 and ran much of its early operations and admissions. Often underweighted in the public telling, she built YC's culture and the founder-empathy that became its signature, and authored the interview book 'Founders at Work'.

She is credited with reading founders' character at YC and launched the annual Female Founders Conference.
RT

Robert Tappan Morris

Co-founder; technical partner (2005–)
2005–

Robert Tappan Morris is an MIT computer science professor and Paul Graham's former Viaweb co-founder, who helped found Y Combinator in 2005. He lent deep technical credibility to YC's early years while remaining a faculty member at MIT.

In 1988 he wrote the Morris Worm, one of the first internet worms, and was the first person convicted under the U.S. Computer Fraud and Abuse Act.
TB

Trevor Blackwell

Co-founder; partner (2005–)
2005–

Trevor Blackwell is a robotics entrepreneur and AI researcher who co-founded Y Combinator in 2005 after earlier working with Paul Graham at Viaweb. Alongside YC he founded the robotics company Anybots, bringing a hardware and engineering perspective to the partnership.

He is known for building a self-balancing robot and an early walking-robot project, blending robotics with early-stage investing.
SA

Sam Altman

President (2014–2019)
2011–2019

Sam Altman went through YC's first batch as co-founder of Loopt, joined as a part-time partner in 2011, and became president in 2014 when Paul Graham stepped back. He scaled YC dramatically — larger batches, hard-tech bets, and the YC Continuity growth fund — turning the accelerator into a multi-stage capital platform.

He left YC in 2019 to focus on OpenAI, where he is CEO and led the launch of ChatGPT.
GT

Garry Tan

President and CEO (2023–)
2023–

Garry Tan is a designer-turned-investor who was an early YC partner and a co-founder of seed fund Initialized Capital before returning to lead Y Combinator as president and CEO in January 2023. He refocused YC on the early stage, wound down its Continuity growth fund, and grew the accelerator's emphasis on AI startups.

As an early investor through Initialized, he backed Coinbase, Instacart, and Flexport, and has appeared on the Forbes Midas List.
MS

Michael Seibel

CEO of the accelerator (2016–2024); group partner
2014–

Michael Seibel co-founded Justin.tv (which became Twitch) and Socialcam, then joined Y Combinator as a partner and ran the core accelerator as its CEO from 2016 to 2024. He shaped much of YC's modern founder education and later returned to a hands-on group-partner role mentoring early-stage startups.

His startup talks and YC's 'How to Start a Startup' material made him one of YC's most-watched mentors; he has served on the boards of Reddit and Dropbox.
PB

Paul Buchheit

Partner (2010–2025); Partner Emeritus
2010–2025

Paul Buchheit was Google's 23rd employee and the creator of Gmail before co-founding FriendFeed and joining Y Combinator as a partner in 2010. As one of YC's first partners beyond the founders, he advised startups for fifteen years before transitioning to Partner Emeritus in 2025.

He created Gmail, built the prototype of AdSense, and is credited with coining Google's motto 'Don't be evil'.
JF

Jared Friedman

Managing Director, Software; group partner
2016–

Jared Friedman co-founded Scribd (YC 2006), which grew into one of the most-visited sites on the web, before joining Y Combinator as a partner. At YC he serves as Managing Director for software and is an active group partner and early-stage angel investor.

He helped popularize YC's 'Requests for Startups' and was an early angel investor in companies including Cruise.
DC

Dalton Caldwell

Managing Director and group partner
2013–

Dalton Caldwell is a music-tech entrepreneur who founded imeem (acquired by MySpace) and App.net before joining Y Combinator as a partner in 2013. As a managing director and group partner he has worked across more than twenty batches, advising over a thousand startups.

He is known inside YC for the concept of 'tarpit ideas' — appealing startup ideas that quietly trap founders.
DH

Diana Hu

Group partner
2020–

Diana Hu was co-founder and CTO of Escher Reality (YC S17), an augmented-reality backend company acquired by Niantic, the maker of Pokémon Go. She later joined Y Combinator as a group partner, bringing deep machine-learning and infrastructure expertise to early-stage technical founders.

After the Niantic acquisition she worked on AR at Niantic before returning to YC to mentor deep-tech founders.
HT

Harj Taggar

Managing partner; YC's first non-founder partner
2010–2014, 2020–

Harj Taggar went through YC as co-founder of Auctomatic (YC W07), then in 2010 became the first partner hired from outside the founding group. He left in 2014 to start Triplebyte (YC S15) and rejoined YC in 2020, where he is a managing partner.

He co-founded Triplebyte and was an early backer at Initialized Capital alongside Garry Tan and Alexis Ohanian.
GR

Geoff Ralston

President (2019–2022); partner
2012–2022

Geoff Ralston led the development of RocketMail (which became Yahoo Mail) and co-founded the edtech incubator Imagine K12 before joining Y Combinator as a partner in 2012. He served as YC's president from 2019 to 2022, steering the firm through the pandemic and fully online, record-sized batches.

As outgoing president he publicly defended keeping YC's standard deal and terms steady even as the market shifted.
AH

Aaron Harris

Partner (2014–2021)
2014–2021

Aaron Harris co-founded Tutorspree (YC S11) and joined Y Combinator as a partner in 2014, where he spent roughly seven and a half years. He worked closely on YC's investment processes and founder support, including helping run the Series A program for alumni.

He co-hosted YC's content and writing on fundraising, and later co-founded the fintech startup Tango.
KH

Kevin Hale

Partner (2013–2020)
2013–2020

Kevin Hale co-founded the online form builder Wufoo, one of the earliest YC-funded startups (acquired by SurveyMonkey in 2011), then became a Y Combinator partner from 2013 to 2020. He focused on user experience, product design, and customer support, helping founders refine their products.

His YC talks on how investors evaluate startup ideas and on conversion and pricing are among YC's most cited.
TB

Tom Blomfield

Group partner (2023–)
2021–

Tom Blomfield is a British entrepreneur who co-founded GoCardless (YC S11) and later the app-based challenger bank Monzo, building two billion-dollar companies. He returned to Y Combinator first as a visiting group partner and became a full group partner in 2023, advising founders on both B2B and consumer startups.

He is one of the rare founders to have scaled two unicorns, GoCardless and Monzo, both rooted in the YC ecosystem.
